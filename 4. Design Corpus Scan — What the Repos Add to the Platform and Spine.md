## The verdict

The three session documents (the platform moonshot, the three-portals tab, and the Open EHR Spine) describe the shape the corpus describes: one record, three faces, an event-driven intelligence layer, evidence linkage, governance as a read model. What the corpus adds is the discipline that makes that shape defensible: a formal unit of decision (the argument object), a single deterministic release gate between every engine and every face, version pinning that guarantees bit-for-bit replay, a typed vocabulary for uncertainty that refuses to let degree, belief, coverage, reliability and fit collapse into one number, a machinery for the system to know and say where it does not apply, a standing adversary, a firewalled evaluation programme, and a regulatory profile split enforced at build time. None of these contradict the session documents; they are the load-bearing members the session documents drew around. Twenty-one clusters are set out below, followed by the repo assets the spine should adopt, and the five places where the session prose is looser than the corpus and should be tightened.

## Additional concepts, by cluster

Each cluster names the corpus IDs it comes from, states the concept in one paragraph, and says where it lands: Platform (the moonshot document), Portals (the three-portals tab) or Spine (the Open EHR Spine document).

### 1. The argument object as the unit of decision (SPINE-1, SPINE-2, SPINE-3, CS-3)

Every claim that crosses a face boundary (recommendation, alert, suppression, override, triage decision) is an ActualArgument instantiating a versioned GenericArgument, carrying all six Toulmin elements: claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier and rebuttal. Qualifier and rebuttal are mandatory. One argument object renders in three registers (clinical, plain-language, compliance) from a shared ontology; renderers may compress but never add, remove or reweight. The session documents speak of "recommendations with evidence links"; the corpus makes the recommendation a typed object with a fixed anatomy, which is what lets the auditor compare what the guideline licensed with what happened at the grain of a single warrant node. Lands in Spine (a first-class resource type beside Observation and MedicationRequest) and Portals (the Argument View).

### 2. ML proposes and tests; only arithmetic releases (SPINE-7, OM-5, RG-1, RG-2, HR-1, HR-4)

No engine, rule-based, Bayesian, fuzzy, ML or LLM, has a path to a face. Engines emit drafts; one deterministic evaluator per deployment runs a five-stage pipeline (completeness, thresholds, envelope, conflicts, verdict) and is the sole path by which any draft becomes face-visible. The evaluator is versioned, exhaustively unit-tested and free of learned parameters. Every verdict (released, held, flagged) is ledgered with its stage trace. Faces render exactly the verdict class: released as content, flagged only through the fit-judgment flow, held never. The session documents' "reasoning layer feeds the workflow layer" becomes "reasoning layer feeds the evaluator, which feeds the workflow layer". Lands in Spine (the evaluator is a core component beside the event stream) and Platform (architecture section).

### 3. Pins and replay (SPINE-5, CP-2, EN-8, RG-4, ME-5, LS-2)

Every argument pins the versions of everything that produced it: guideline lineage, terminology release, evidence-library snapshot, codebook, envelope, engine model and configuration hash, compiler version. Any historical decision is replayable bit-for-bit. One lifecycle replay harness serves every change class: a model, curve, template, ontology or terminology change replays a sentinel decision set and produces a divergence report before ratification. The Spine document's provenance model already stores author, time, source and model version; the corpus extends that to a full pin set and makes replay a gate. Lands in Spine (provenance section) and Portals (governance).

### 4. Deviation as a first-class, non-punitive act; compliance vocabulary reframed (SPINE-8, CF-3, HA-2, AF-3, AR-2, MA-4, MA-6)

A clinician can depart from any GenericArgument; the departure is a Deviation object with a structured reason taxonomy, free text, severity tier and author, opened in one interaction with an explicit preview of how it will appear to the auditor. The compliance vocabulary distinguishes guideline-concordant, documented justified deviation (a compliant state, not a mitigated violation), documented deviation under review, and undocumented deviation, extended by in-envelope and out-of-envelope release states. Meta-rational acts never feed individual performance management by default. The Portals tab describes "justified versus unjustified deviation" informally; the corpus supplies the object, the friction law and the vocabulary that a payer or accreditor can be asked to accept. Lands in Portals (clinician acts, governance vocabulary) and Spine (Deviation resource).

### 5. The five-signal registry (OM-3, HR-2, CV-1, CV-2, FS-3)

Posterior (belief), coverage (conformal set and stated coverage), membership (graded degree with term label), reliability (source-stated confidence) and fit (envelope status) are five distinct, non-coercible types from schema through transport to render. No engine, aggregation or renderer sums them, converts one into another, or shows a composite score. Degree never wears verdict's clothes: membership renders as chip plus term plus glanceable graphic, never traffic-light. The session documents use "confidence" loosely throughout; the corpus forbids that word for membership or fit. Lands in Platform (capability catalogue), Portals (every rendering) and Spine (the typed signal fields on the argument resource).

### 6. The linguistic layer: vague terms as governed artefacts (FS-1 to FS-9, FC-1 to FC-7, FP-1 to FP-8, FE-1 to FE-9, FX-1 to FX-3)

Every clinical vague term the system computes over ("elevated", "severe", "borderline") is a LinguisticVariable artefact with a universe of discourse, term set, membership functions, permitted hedges, authorship and evidence backing, serialised in IEEE 1855 FML. Grounds carry membership vectors pinned to the curve version. Each register has a ratified codebook; the plain register speaks words and pictures, never scores. Patient-reported grounds carry a Z-ground schema: value plus optional reliability from a small ratified vocabulary, offered by a reliability dial, never demanded. Patients may calibrate their own scale usage (the PIS profile), visible and revocable. Boundary proximity is an event: a value within a ratified borderline band flags the case, shows distance to threshold and offers the deviation sheet. Curve change flows only through a workbench with a human-readable FML diff and sentinel replay. None of this is in the session documents. Lands in Platform (a new capability row), Portals (patient intake and clinician chips) and Spine (LinguisticVariable and codebook as knowledge-plane resources; membership annotations on observations).

### 7. Meta-rationality: the system knows where it does not apply (MS-1 to MS-9, MC-1 to MC-7, MP-1 to MP-6, MA-1 to MA-7, ME-1 to ME-8, MX-1 to MX-5)

Every formal element carries a machine-readable ApplicabilityEnvelope: the population it was validated for, its stated exclusions and known failure regions. Every rendered recommendation shows its envelope status for this patient (in, out with named attributes, unknown), at the recommendation's own visual weight. Any actor, clinician, patient or auditor, can file a GapReport ("this does not fit", "this doesn't describe me") on any instrument item or rendered argument in one interaction, and the report carries authorship into the fabric. Circumrational load (override rates, free-text supplement volume, "none of these" selections, abandoned forms) is measured as the translation work humans do at the system's boundary. Ontological remodeling follows one governed lifecycle: detect, propose, deliberate in a chartered trading zone, ratify, version, replay. Co-resident guideline systems that conflict produce a ConflictRecord rendered side by side; the clinician's navigation is a recorded act and the system never pre-ranks the sides. Every face can answer "what is this system validated for, what is it bad at, what changed recently" from fabric data. The session documents treat applicability implicitly ("where the pathway and the patient diverge it says so"); the corpus makes it a typed envelope with a gap-report loop and an equity lens over gap clusters. Lands in all three, and is the single largest addition.

### 8. The standing adversary (EN-5, AD-1 to AD-5, FE-8, ME-2, Primer G)

A corruption engine generates guaranteed-wrong test material by breaking known-good content along clinically meaningful boundaries (a tenfold dose, a swapped unit, a likelihood ratio flipped across one, a dropped contraindication edge), so the label is true by construction. It runs three target maps on scheduled campaigns: membership geometry, ontology edges, and calibration and coverage. Confirmed findings publish as rebuttal objects bound to the warrants they defeat and are face-visible wherever those warrants fire; a version with unacknowledged findings cannot release. Campaign-coverage telemetry records when each warrant, curve, envelope and engine version was last attacked, and never-attacked surface renders on the governance lens. The session documents mention "drift monitoring"; the corpus adds an adversary that is itself measured. Lands in Platform (gates section) and Portals (governance watchfulness).

### 9. The evaluation firewall and living evaluation (EN-7, RG-3, Primer C, Primer I, Primer H)

Training and tuning corpora are disjoint from scoring corpora; evaluation code is versioned; every reported figure is reproducible by a party with no engine write access. The casebundle corpus is an independent examination instrument whose entire value is that no system under test has learned from it, spent only at formal checkpoints. Living evaluation replaces fixed regression with metamorphic invariants asserted over freshly generated cases every run (adding a red-flag finding never lowers acuity; removing evidence widens, never narrows, the conformal set; paraphrase never changes the differential) and library self-consistency (test presentations regenerate from the current evidence library each release). A staged national data-linkage validation pathway anchors the core calibration claim to real outcomes without data ever leaving governance. The Platform document's "evaluation" gate is one paragraph; the corpus is a programme. Lands in Platform (gates) and Portals (governance research and evaluation).

### 10. Conformal qualifiers, subgroup coverage, abstention and deferral (EN-4, QU-1 to QU-5, DX-5, DX-6)

Every probabilistic claim carries a conformal prediction set with stated coverage, computed by a wrapper validated on the firewalled corpus per version. Coverage is validated and monitored group-conditionally over a ratified subgroup schema. Abstention is a first-class output: "insufficient grounds to rank" with the missing-grounds list renders as gather-more-information recommendations rather than a forced ranking. Selective prediction with cost-aware deferral is the preferred action policy on low-confidence and out-of-envelope cases. Qualifier telemetry (realised coverage by subgroup, set sizes, abstention and deferral rates) flows to governance and sustained degradation is a governance event with an owner. Lands in Platform (capability catalogue, risk stratification row) and Portals (clinician differential and governance).

### 11. Governed clinical content: evidence library, content registry, graph retrieval (Primers B, D, E)

Diagnosis-side evidence lives in a structured library (per-condition priors, presentation variants, discriminating findings with sensitivity, specificity and likelihood ratios, pathognomonic and rule-out findings, patient-facing questions) with a sourced evidence table behind every number and a validator enforcing likelihood-ratio arithmetic. Treatment-side content lives in a content registry of signed fragments, each carrying content hash, source identity and version, effective and review dates, evidence tier, jurisdiction and machine-readable dose bounds per agent, route and age band, with AMT coding of every medication; changes go through a content-as-code repository with pharmacist and clinician code-owners, signed commits and CI release gates. Retrieval is a knowledge graph whose edges (first-line-for, contraindicated-in, interacts-with, monitor-with) may exist only if an authoritative fragment asserts them, with hybrid graph-plus-embedding retrieval and contraindication pruning. The Spine document's "evidence and knowledge index" becomes three governed stores with different write disciplines. Lands in Spine (knowledge layer).

### 12. The guideline compiler and plural lineages (EN-3, CP-1 to CP-6, SPINE-6, XC-4, PF-7, TL-4, ME-3)

One compilation path carries all clinical logic: narrative guideline to computable form (CQL, PlanDefinition, WHO SMART machine-readable layer) to GenericArgument templates bearing evidence-tier backing per warrant node. Hand-coded rules outside the compiler are prohibited. Recompilation is at criterion granularity with a diff and impact preview. Jurisdictional adaptations compile as sibling lineages with their own ratification trails and co-reside without merging; conflicts materialise as DetectedIssue objects. The compiler records each guideline's ontological commitments (scope, exclusions) as machine-readable envelope content. The Spine document says "guideline pathways as executable, versioned logic with clinical owners"; the corpus specifies the only door they may enter through. Lands in Spine (knowledge layer, compiler as a component).

### 13. Attention governance (HG-1 to HG-5, CF-4, CF-5, CC-2, CA-4)

One attention budget governs all interruption classes (alerts, borderline flags, meta-prompts, fit warnings) per encounter and per clinician, with ratified class weights and fabric-visible spend. Hard stops are reserved for the deterministic safety class (arithmetic contraindication with ratified backing); everything else is advisory and dismissible. Suppression is governed: a suppression rule is proposed, argued, ratified as a GenericArgument, versioned and its firings logged; silent suppression is prohibited. Interruptions fire only on fabric-grounded triggers, never on schedule or engagement. The clinician sees their own budget spend and the suppression rules affecting them (My Attention), the same numbers governance sees. The Portals tab says "alert fatigue is managed explicitly"; the corpus supplies the mechanism. Lands in Portals (clinician) and Platform (workflow layer).

### 14. Watchfulness: theatre detection, argued metrics, anytime-valid monitoring (AF-4, AT-1 to AT-5, MA-3, AE-2)

Theatre and gaming detection runs continuously over the justification ledger: boilerplate similarity, duplication clusters, temporal anomalies, taxonomy-versus-free-text divergence; flags go to human review and never auto-sanction. Every metric the governance face computes (compliance rates, queue ages, drift indices, detector precision) is itself a versioned, argued artefact with a stated purpose and known failure modes, changed only through the governed lifecycle. Anytime-valid methods (conformal test martingales) are the preferred mathematics for continuous streams so alarm rates are controlled. Detector performance is measured from review outcomes. Lands in Portals (governance).

### 15. Regulator export, obligations register, reconstruction validation, dispute mode (AF-7, AX-1 to AX-4, AE-1, AE-3, AF-6, AR-4, TA-5)

Regulator export produces self-contained conformity bundles: the decision set with pinned versions, replay attestation, argument transparency, deviation states and adverse-event linkage, plus the meta-level bundle (remodeling ledger extracts, gap analytics, envelope-compliance states, self-description at pinned versions). Every projection exports its flattening, the mapping from fabric states to any external vocabulary. The obligations register is evidenced from the fabric with a named owner and a standing evidence query per obligation. Founding validation is reconstruction: an external reviewer, from exports alone, reconstructs a month of decisions. Institutional acceptance of "documented justified deviation" as compliant is studied with a real payer, not assumed. Dispute mode is a structured online-dispute-resolution workflow usable for payer disputes, patient record challenges and inter-clinician escalation, with patient-initiated entry from the patient face. The Portals tab has "reporting with lineage"; the corpus has the bundle format and the validation that proves it. Lands in Portals (governance) and Spine (bulk export).

### 16. The low-resource floor as a release gate (PF-6, XC-3, TL-1 to TL-5, PA-1 to PA-6, PV-1 to PV-5, PS-1 to PS-6, PI-1 to PI-5)

Usable at low literacy and low computer literacy, WCAG 2.2 AA, functional offline with deferred sync, with stated bandwidth and device floors tested per release. Linguistic intake is the primary low-resource modality: word-chips over keypads, IVR menus that speak codebook terms, SMS parity for intake, reminders, escalation and gap reporting. Every screen passes the tired-thumb test (one primary action reachable one-handed on the smallest device). Capture is resumable and lossless across interruption and app death. Notifications follow a payload law (task prompts and acknowledgments only; no clinical content crosses the bright line) under a patient-set budget. Helper mode records assisted entry by a family member or community health worker. Language packs install per jurisdiction under lineage rules. The Portals tab's patient portal assumes a connected smartphone; the corpus designs for the person who does not have one. Lands in Portals (patient) and Spine (edge and offline).

### 17. Regulatory profiles enforced at build time (GPP-1 to GPP-16, RG-6, FX-2, XC-1, XC-2, AN-1 to AN-12)

Three profiles of one codebase: J-1 (lower-class included, deterministic runtime), J-2 (higher-class included, ML runtime) and J-3 (a guideline-prompt profile that tests the CDSS exemption honestly). J-3 diverges by exclusion only: the modules for Bayesian inference, conformal wrapping, LLM runtime, device-signal ingestion and prohibited claim types are absent from the compiled artefact; the schema rejects any warrant type other than guideline-rule; a conformance suite attempts each prohibited capability and must fail; the artefact cryptographically attests its profile at startup and a spine-level boundary monitor alarms on any out-of-profile claim. Enabling any excluded capability is supply of a different device. Tier placement of every engine is a build-time property with SBOM evidence. The regulatory posture is a single citation surface with an obligations register, watch programme, gates, and a claims boundary that marketing and in-product copy must match exactly. The Spine document's "compile-time compliance" gestures at this; the corpus specifies it. Lands in Spine (security and compliance) and Platform (gates).

### 18. Workflow placement: capture before, synthesise before, decide during (CF-1, HW-1 to HW-5, CS-1, PF-1, TW-1)

Data capture happens before the consultation on the patient face with point-of-entry validation; synthesis happens before the encounter (the Consult-Prep Composer); in-consultation interaction is read-and-decide. The brief is bounded by a ratified reading budget (a ninety-second core read) and overflow collapses behind drill-down; adding a new signal class never grows the surface. In-consultation input is confined to the recorded acts: confirm, sign off, deviate, report gap, judge fit, navigate conflict, annotate. The Portals tab has the pre-visit brief but also describes live differential updating during the consultation; the corpus places synthesis before the encounter and treats the consultation as decision time. See the disagreements section. Lands in Portals (clinician).

### 19. Fail-closed sign-off and the bright line (HA-1, CS-5, PF-8, TR-3, PS-3, PS-4)

No recommendation becomes an order, prescription, referral or patient-visible diagnostic statement without an attributed clinician sign-off recorded in the fabric; the Sign-off Bar is the only release control and states exactly what will be released, to whom, in which register, at which argument version. The patient face reflects the patient's own observations immediately but renders diagnostic claims, risk statements and recommendations only after clinician release, and no patient-reachable surface (screen, preview, notification, widget, share card, email digest, IVR prompt) can carry pre-release clinical content. The Spine document's order lifecycle has "signed"; the corpus makes sign-off the terminal act of the face and the bright line structural in the UI. Lands in Portals (both faces) and Spine (order lifecycle).

### 20. Team modes and disagreement held, not averaged (CF-6, HT-1 to HT-4, CS-6)

Group decisions (multidisciplinary meetings, ward rounds, asynchronous specialist input, low-resource tele-consultation) are captured as multi-author arguments preserving who contributed which grounds and warrants and where disagreement remained; the face never renders a group output as unanimous when the fabric knows otherwise. Where ratified meanings carry community bands, team views render the spread, not a false point. Not in the session documents. Lands in Portals (clinician) and Spine (multi-author argument resource).

### 21. Patient custody, patient council, and separated secondary-use consent (PF-3, PF-4, TA-2, TA-4, TC-1 to TC-4, MP-3, MP-6, TW-5)

Patient values and priorities are patient-authored structured data whose mapping to clinical weightings takes effect only after ratification through the full remodeling lifecycle with the patient as a party who can see, contest and revoke at every stage. Patient-generated metadata (calibration profiles, reliability components, gap reports, values structures) is patient-custodied: visible in their data view, exported with their record, revocable where they granted it. Repository routing (where the data lives, under which jurisdiction) is policy-driven, explainable in plain language and ledgered. Secondary-use consent is separate, specific and never bundled with care consent; declining changes nothing about care. Patient-affecting changes pass a patient-council stage in deliberation. The Portals tab has consent and access ledgers; the corpus adds custody of derived data, the council and the separation rule. Lands in Portals (patient) and Spine (consent).

## Where the session documents and the corpus disagree

Five places where the session prose is looser than the corpus. Each is stated once with the reconciliation; the corpus position is the filed one and the session documents should be tightened to it unless ruled otherwise.

**Live differential during the consultation versus synthesis before it.** The Portals tab describes a working differential that updates as the transcript unfolds, with next-best questions offered in the room. The corpus places synthesis before the encounter (CF-1, HW-1) and confines in-consultation input to the recorded acts (CI-2). Reconciliation: ambient capture and extraction may run live and enrich the record, but the differential the clinician sees is the evaluator-released Consult-Prep projection; any in-consultation re-ranking is a new draft that must pass the evaluator before it renders, and the reading budget (HW-3) governs what is shown. Next-best-question logic survives as gather-more-information recommendations (DX-6), which are released claim types.

**Engines feeding the workflow directly versus one gate.** The Platform and Spine documents draw reasoning feeding workflow, and describe risk re-scoring that "alerts if threshold crossed". The corpus has no engine with a path to a face (OM-5); every alert is a released argument with a stage trace (RG-1, RG-2). Reconciliation: insert the deterministic evaluator between the reasoning layer and the workflow layer in both documents, and make every alert, task and draft an argument object.

**LLM in the differential.** The Platform document's capability catalogue and the Portals tab allow language models to participate in differential reasoning and drafting during the consultation. The corpus excludes LLM runtime inference from posterior computation, ranking and release in every tier (DX-7), permits LLM work as authoring-time proposers under ratification (EN-6, CP-5), and reserves runtime LLM capabilities for the classified J-2 track with the nine named capabilities. Reconciliation: label every LLM use in the session documents by class; summarisation, record question-answering and drafting for signature remain permitted where the output is derived content marked as such and never a released clinical claim.

**"Confidence" as a single number.** The session documents use confidence and probability vocabulary for extraction certainty, model output and applicability alike. The corpus forbids collapsing the five signals (OM-3) and lints microcopy for the prohibited vocabulary (CV-4, FC-6). Reconciliation: wherever the session documents say confidence, name the signal: extraction reliability, posterior, coverage, membership or fit.

**Alerts as interruptions versus an attention budget.** The session documents manage alert fatigue by "tuning thresholds per role". The corpus governs all interruption classes under one budget with ratified weights, reserves hard stops for arithmetic contraindication, and requires suppression rules to be ratified arguments (HG-1 to HG-3). Reconciliation: replace threshold tuning with the budget and the governed-suppression machinery.
